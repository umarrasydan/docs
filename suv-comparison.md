# 6/7-Seater SUV Comparison — Malaysia Market (2025)

Cars compared: **Hyundai Santa Fe**, **Zeekr**, **Volvo XC90**, **Proton X90**.

Requirement: **more than 5 seats**.

> **Note on Zeekr:** Zeekr's SUV in Malaysia — the Zeekr 7X — is only a 5-seater, so it does not meet the seating requirement. The only Zeekr in Malaysia with 6+ seats is the **Zeekr 009** (a luxury EV MPV, not an SUV). Both are listed below so you can decide.

## 2. Engine & powertrain

### Proton X90 — 1.5T + 48V mild hybrid
- 1,477 cc 3-cylinder turbo petrol + 48 V belt-starter generator.
- Combined ~190 hp, 300 Nm, 7-speed DCT, FWD.
- Mild hybrid — the electric motor assists but cannot drive the car alone.
- Fine for cruising and highway; some reviewers report hesitation and low-speed DCT jerk.

### Hyundai Santa Fe — choose hybrid or petrol AWD
- **Prime / Prestige:** 1.6T "THEV" full hybrid — 235 PS / 367 Nm, FWD, 6-speed auto. Strong real-world economy (~6 L/100 km).
- **Calligraphy:** 2.5T GDi petrol — 281 PS / 422 Nm, 8-speed DCT, AWD. Most performance, highest running cost.
- Same boxy new-gen chassis, 6- or 7-seat layouts.

### Zeekr 009 Executive — pure electric luxury MPV
- Front-motor FWD, 335 hp / 373 Nm, 116 kWh NMC battery, 604 km WLTP.
- Luxury / Ultra Luxury AWD dual-motor trims push 603 hp and 0–100 in 4.5 s.
- 150 kW DC fast charge (10–80% in ~30 min), free wallbox included in launch package.
- Massive torque, silent cabin, but range drops significantly with heavy highway use.

### Volvo XC90 — mild hybrid or plug-in hybrid
- **B5 MHEV:** 2.0T + 48 V, 249 hp, AWD, 8-speed auto.
- **T8 PHEV:** 2.0T + electric motor, 462 hp combined, ~70 km pure-electric range, AWD.
- PHEV gives huge torque + EV commuting without range anxiety — but only if you actually charge it.

**Engine verdict**
- Want lowest running cost without a charger → **Santa Fe 1.6 hybrid**.
- Want pure EV and can charge at home → **Zeekr 009**.
- Want best of both worlds and have a charger → **XC90 T8**.
- Budget-first, cheap to buy and run on petrol → **Proton X90**.

## 3. Reliability

| | Rating (external) | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Proton X90 | Too new for long-term data | Launched 2023. Geely-shared platform with Lynk & Co 01. Owners report generally reliable; some DCT jerkiness at low speed. Good Malaysian service network and cheap parts. |
| Hyundai Santa Fe | Strong — higher resale than XC90 (+10.4 pp), TrueCar owner avg 4.86/5 | 8-year/160,000 km Malaysian warranty is the longest here. New 5th-gen so early-adopter risk exists, but Hyundai's recent drivetrain record is solid. |
| Zeekr 009 | No independent long-term data | EV drivetrains are mechanically simpler, but software, 12 V systems and HV battery long-term costs are the unknowns. After-sales in Malaysia is still growing — only a handful of service centres. |
| Volvo XC90 | RepairPal 3.5/5 (8th of 14 luxury mid-size SUVs); avg US repair ~USD 851/yr | Can last 200,000+ miles with care. Parts and labour are the most expensive of the four in Malaysia. |

**Reliability verdict**
- Lowest ownership risk on paper: **Santa Fe** (longest warranty, proven hybrid).
- Lowest repair cost in Malaysia: **Proton X90** (Proton parts, wide network).
- Most expensive to fix if something breaks: **Volvo XC90**.
- Biggest unknown: **Zeekr 009** — very new to Malaysia, EV-specific risk.

## 4. Value for money

Price-to-feature, Malaysian context:

### Proton X90 — best raw value
- Sub-RM150k for a 7-seat SUV with ADAS (AEB, FCW), panoramic roof, Nappa leather (Flagship), 14-speaker audio.
- Cheapest to buy, tax, insure, service.
- Trade-off: lower resale vs. Hyundai, uninspiring 1.5T performance, non-premium badge.

### Hyundai Santa Fe — best balanced value
- Pricing jumps to RM225k but you get the 8-year warranty, full hybrid with genuine fuel savings, strong resale, and arguably the most spacious third row of the group.
- The 1.6T hybrid Prime (RM225k) is the sweet spot. Calligraphy (RM270k) is more about AWD and captain chairs than raw value.

### Zeekr 009 — best "wow factor" per ringgit, but not cheap
- RM299,800 for a 335 hp EV MPV with 604 km range, 150 kW charging, and genuinely S-Class-level rear seating — undercuts European rivals (Mercedes EQV, BMW i5 MPV equivalents) by RM100k+.
- Running cost is very low if you charge at home.
- Depreciation risk is the biggest unknown — Chinese EV residuals in Malaysia are still being established.

### Volvo XC90 — premium badge, weakest value
- Starts RM418,888 — roughly **double** the Santa Fe and **3.4× the X90**.
- You pay for brand, safety reputation, build quality, and the B&W audio / air suspension on Ultra.
- The 5-year unlimited-km warranty is good, but service costs are noticeably higher.

**Value verdict**
| You care most about… | Pick |
|---|---|
| Lowest upfront + running cost | Proton X90 |
| Best overall ownership balance | Hyundai Santa Fe (1.6 hybrid) |
| Premium EV, no range anxiety, home charging | Zeekr 009 |
| Premium badge, luxury brand, resale in that segment | Volvo XC90 |

---

## 5. Recommendations

- **Family on a budget (≤ RM150k):** Proton X90 Flagship.
- **Best all-round family SUV (RM200k–270k):** Hyundai Santa Fe 1.6 Hybrid Prestige.
- **Future-proof EV, luxury chauffeur feel:** Zeekr 009 Executive.
- **Safety-first premium, long-term keep:** Volvo XC90 B5 MHEV (or T8 if you'll charge daily).
